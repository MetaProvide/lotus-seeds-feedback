1Before 7 October

Two things to build

Building the real thing is the test that tells us whether Lotus works. These two are the most useful thing you can send us this month.

1Events

Create a real recurring event

Pick one you actually run, and make it a recurring one — a weekly class, a monthly retreat, whatever repeats at your centre. Use your real sessions, formats, teacher, capacity and prices. Then register for it yourself, as a participant would, all the way to the end.

Book as an ordinary participant this round; membership pricing joins that path next.

Then go back and change something on the recurring event — swap the teacher, or add an extra session — and read what the propagation summary tells you it is about to do across the occurrences. That is the piece we most need tested.

2Memberships

Create a real membership

Build the membership program you would actually sell: your real tier names, your real prices, your real benefits, and which events each tier includes. Publish it.

Nothing you publish charges anyone yet, so you can shape it freely — and you will find out fast whether your tiers fit. Publishing locks a plan in, so choose as if it were live.

Why the membership one matters most

Payments arrive next, and once a member is charged their terms are frozen. This month is the cheap moment to get the shape of your program exactly how you want it.

Send us one thing you noticed

One honest observation from each of you is enough to change the next cycle. The feedback form or the channel, whichever is quicker for you.

5What we need from you

Four questions

The right answer differs for each centre, so yours is the one that counts — and these are the choices that are cheapest to make now. Answer in the channel, in the feedback form, or by email. A rough answer today beats a considered one in December.

1

Your tables

Look at the tables in the admin backend — events, registrations, people, memberships. Which fields or filters would let you export exactly the information you need?

Be specific: the column your treasurer asks for, the filter you would use every Monday morning, the thing you currently rebuild by hand in a spreadsheet.

2

Changing membership terms

When you raise a price or change what is included, what should happen to people who already joined? Stay where they are indefinitely, move at their next renewal, or be asked?

And what notice do you feel you owe them?

3

Included, or discounted?

Jamyang's model is inclusive: a member simply pays nothing for an included event. IVY's card model is a percentage off. Lotus does the inclusive model today, and percentages are ours to build if you need them.

If we build percentages, they would apply to events first — courses, lodging and the rest would follow later. If your centre needs them, saying so now is far cheaper than later.

4

Your back office

For staff we plan to build activate, pause, resume and cancel. Renewing, upgrading and editing payment details stay with the member, in their own account.

Our instinct is that a member is the one who changes what they are billed. Two look like real exceptions: cancel, because someone will always phone instead, and refund, which we probably do need to build.

Does that match a real week at your centre, or is there a case where you genuinely have to act in a member's name?

And one for Jamyang: your Calm Abiding and Special Insight students are already enrolled and paying. When memberships arrive, what should they be offered?
