How did the testing go?

On 2 September we asked you for two things: build a real recurring event and register for it, and build the membership program you would actually sell.

  • The event: did the change summary match what you meant to change? Where did you have to guess?
  • The membership: did your tiers fit? Was anything about your real program impossible to express?
  • If you stopped somewhere: where exactly? That point is worth more to us than anything on the rest of these slides.
  • If you did not get to it: was it time, or was it not knowing where to start? Both are ours to fix, not yours.
No wrong answers. Silence is the only outcome we cannot learn from.

What we shipped · 1 of 4 · You told us

Creating an event is too hard. We heard you.

The clearest message from your testing: building an event takes too long, and when something goes wrong it is not clear why. We have improved a lot, and we are not finished.

Fewer errors, and clearer ones

“Can’t publish” now tells you the cause and the dates it affects, instead of a generic message. Tooltips explain session types, schedules, intervals and booking options. Clearer buttons for adding things.

Not losing your work

We are fixing the cases where an error cleared a form you had filled in, and adding a summary step so you can check everything before creating the event.

Duplicate an event

Next on the list: copy an existing event and change only the dates and the teacher, instead of building it again from the start.

Templates, being considered

Thubten Norbu Ling runs over a thousand events a year from a catalogue. We are looking at event templates so a repeat event takes a minute, not half an hour.

Still working on it — tell us where it still hurts

What we shipped · 2 of 4 · The big one

Payments, first version

The feature everything else was waiting for. A participant can now register and pay, and the money goes straight to your center.

Straight into your Stripe account

Each center connects its own Stripe account and is paid directly. Lotus never holds your money. Card payments at checkout, handled by Stripe.

What your team can do

See every order and payment, issue a full or partial refund, and send a payment link to someone who could not pay online at the time.

What a participant sees

A checkout after registering, and a My orders page showing what they have paid and what is still due.

An initial version, honestly

We tested it end to end on 1 October and are closing the final adjustments now. Next week it is yours to test — which is exactly what we will ask you at the end.

What we shipped · 3 of 4

Memberships, more consolidated

In September you saw the half that does not involve money. That half is now complete, and joining with a payment comes with the payments work.

For your members

Public membership pages, a signup flow, and a place in My Lotus to see and manage their membership: pause, resume, cancel, or change plan.

For your team

The same actions from the admin side, and versions you can publish, archive and copy — so raising a price never quietly re-prices the people who already joined.

Benefits that work at registration

A member registering for an included event pays nothing, automatically. Courses included in a membership open up for members too.

What comes next is yours

The next improvements come from your feedback on the program you built. That is why the membership you create matters.

What we shipped · 4 of 4

Moodle, running more smoothly

Small changes, each one removing a way the connection could quietly do the wrong thing.

Your students’ details stay yours

Signing in through Moodle no longer overwrites a student’s profile in Lotus.

Access that adds up

Granting course access twice no longer leaves someone with access after it is revoked. Each registration now shows its course access, with a link between the two screens.

A registration survives a Moodle hiccup

If Moodle refuses an enrolment, the registration is kept and retried, rather than lost.

Safer to set up, and to switch off

Pairing refuses an address that is not a Moodle site. Disconnecting Moodle keeps your registrations readable, and we have written down exactly what it does.

The part that is not features

Security and privacy, nearly there

In September we promised your boards real answers. Here is where each one stands.

Independent security review

Done. It found six things to fix. The first is already fixed, and the rest are being fixed now.

Cyber Essentials

Almost submitted. Every required control is in place on our servers, and the firewall review is signed. Then a recognized certificate your board can point to.

GDPR and the legal documents

On their third version. The external legal review is done, and we are working through its comments now.

What that means for your center

A data processing agreement, service agreement and participant terms you can hand to your own lawyer, and a security story that has been checked by someone other than us.

And quietly in the background

Firewalls on every server, regular backups, and personal accounts for everyone who administers them.
