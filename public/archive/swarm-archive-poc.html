    How preservation works

    Addressed by what it contains

    Each teaching goes up as one folder — audio, transcript, metadata — and gets a single address calculated from the contents themselves. Change one character of the transcript and the address changes with it. Anyone can resolve that address without Lotus and without the source archive, which is what makes it a citation rather than a link.

    Split up and spread across the network

    The teaching is not filed on one server. Swarm cuts it into 4 KB pieces and scatters them across thousands of independent nodes worldwide, each piece landing near nodes whose addresses are closest to its own. Every piece is held by several nodes at once, and the network keeps replacing copies as machines come and go. Losing the teaching would take a large, simultaneous failure across the whole network — not one company shutting down, one server dying, or one budget being cut.

    One stamp keeps it all alive

    Every file in the archive is covered by a single prepaid postage batch — the stamp shown above. Storage is rent, not an endowment: the batch holds a balance that drains a little with every block, and when it empties the nodes holding those pieces are free to discard them. So the archive stays alive by being topped up. MetaProvide pays for that, and every record shows the date it is funded through, because a preservation promise with no funding date behind it is not a promise.

    Two consequences worth being plain about. Because the address comes from the content, the archive is tamper-evident rather than tamper-proof — nobody can alter a teaching in place, but a new version is simply a different address. And because a stamp can lapse, “permanent” is a word we avoid; what Swarm actually buys is independence from any single organisation, including ours.

    Nothing about a person is stored here. The archive holds published teachings only. Registrations, attendance and any record identifying a living participant stay in Lotus, where they can be corrected, exported and erased.
